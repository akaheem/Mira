How Mira works, and where the data comes from

The problem Mira is aimed at

Support for students usually exists. It is just hard to find, hard to trust, and scattered across noticeboards, handbooks and word of mouth. The student who most needs the fee waiver is often the one with the least time to work out which office to visit and what to bring. That gap falls hardest on students who already have the least margin — which is why it is a sustainable-development problem and not just a design one.

Mira answers three questions

I need something
Which nearby place has what you need, and is it open right now.
I need help with a problem
Which office handles it, what to bring, what happens in what order, and how long it should take.
Get me there
What the trip costs, how long it takes, and whether women's transport runs when you travel.

Three kinds of entry, and they are not interchangeable

This is the part that matters most, so it is stated plainly rather than left to a badge. Every entry in Mira carries one of three states, and the state is a field in the data — not something the interface guesses from whether a link happens to be present.

  • ✓ Verified

    A real-world claim, and the cited source supports this specific claim, within the jurisdiction stated and the relevant time period. A source that proves something related is not enough.

  • ◇ Illustrative

    Intentionally fictional information, created to demonstrate the product. It describes no real place and no real service, and is never presented as a real-world fact.

  • ! Not yet verified

    A real-world claim for which Mira does not currently have adequate evidence. It is not presented as established fact, and it is not used as the basis for any claim about impact.

A record is only marked Verified when the declared state and a real citation agree. If a record claims to be verified and carries no source, Mira shows it as Not yet verified and reports the inconsistency: the safe direction to be wrong in is under-claiming, never over-claiming.

Where a fact applies only to one country, it is tagged with that country. Provenance and jurisdiction are separate fields on purpose: a verified legal process in one country must never read as a universal rule for students everywhere.

What Mira deliberately does not do

  • It does not rate a route as safe or unsafe. It cannot verify that, and a score would hide the fact.
  • It does not invent a process. Answers come from a fixed set of documented processes, and the matcher only ever chooses between them.
  • It does not promote a fictional entry to Verified because something similar exists in the real world. A real source establishes that a service exists there, not that it exists at the place shown.
  • It does not replace an office or an adviser. It gets you to the right door with the right papers.

What happens to what you type

Stated as precisely as the implementation allows, because a privacy claim broader than the system can keep is worse than no claim at all.

  • Mira requires no account, and there is no login.
  • What you type is sent once, over HTTPS, to select a process. It is matched by this application's own code against a fixed list of topics. It is never sent to an AI service or any third party.
  • It is not written to any store Mira controls — no database, no file, no cache keyed on it. Once the response is rendered, the server holds no copy.
  • The application emits no log line containing it, and no analytics event. It is submitted by POST rather than in the URL, so it does not land in browser history or in a proxy's access log.
  • The limit of that promise: Mira runs on a hosting platform that keeps its own standard request logs, which this project does not control. So Mira does not claim that nothing is recorded anywhere — only that Mira itself stores nothing, and that no copy is kept beyond the response you are reading.
  • Journey sharing works the opposite way around: the summary is assembled in your own browser and handed to your phone's share sheet. It is never sent to Mira at all.

How it is built

A small FastAPI application rendering server-side templates, deployed as a serverless function. Data lives in plain files behind a single repository interface, so moving to a database later is a change to one module rather than the whole application. It is built mobile-first, because the student who needs it is holding a phone.

The application is location-parameterised: every record carries its own country, city and campus, and a new location is new data rows rather than new code. The two locations included here are a demonstration of that, not the intended scope.

Which SDG targets this maps to

Target-level, not theme-level. Mira does not claim to have measured impact against any of these; these are the targets the problem itself sits inside.

11.2
Safe, affordable and accessible transport, “with special attention to the needs of those in vulnerable situations, women, children…” — the journey flow.
5.2
Eliminating all forms of violence against all women and girls — the harassment process, which is why it is sourced to the statute that governs it.
4.5
Eliminating gender disparities in education — the awareness gap that keeps support unclaimed falls hardest on those with the least margin.
10.2
Promoting social and economic inclusion irrespective of sex or economic status — the fee, scholarship and food routes.
4.3
Equal access to affordable quality tertiary education — the overall aim.
